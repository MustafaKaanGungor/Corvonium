What changes, and what doesn't

The real win

The matrix becomes a matrix

On a phone the four groups have to stack into a list, and headers carry the meaning. With width, position carries it instead — urgent along one axis, important along the other, exactly the diagram the method is named for.

Shell only

Top bar, no swipe

The three destinations move into a top bar with sync state and settings alongside. Month navigation becomes arrow buttons, because there is no gesture left to spend on it.

Sheets become panels

Detail beside, not on top

The day view and the item editor slide in from the right, so the calendar or list you came from stays visible next to what you are reading.

Constraint lifted

Eight bricks, not three

The ceiling that shapes every decision on the phone mostly dissolves — a recurring standup can appear on all five weekdays without pushing anything into "+N more".

Unchanged

Everything under the shell

Same collections, same derived state, the same single item editor. Missed and urgent are still read off the clock. The desktop build knows nothing the phone doesn't.

Still open

The keyboard

A desktop planner wants shortcuts and a command palette — jump to a date, capture a task without reaching for the mouse. Not drawn here, and worth its own pass.
